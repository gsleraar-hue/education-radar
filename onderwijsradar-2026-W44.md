## Week in one glance
The Dutch government is moving from AI guidance to AI infrastructure for schools, while US survey data shows growing teacher scepticism about classroom technology ("techlash"). Teacher-workforce policy is in the spotlight in both the Netherlands and England, where long-promised measures are being evaluated or delayed rather than delivered.

## Top 3

### 1. Netherlands plans a public "AI hub" for primary and secondary schools (~13 Oct)
**What happened.** State Secretary Tielen told the Tweede Kamer (letter of 13 October) that the cabinet wants a public AI environment for po/vo ready in 2028: an "AI hub" acting as a layer between education applications and different AI models, with a model register and safeguards. €10.7m is available next year plus €7.4m structural; a plan of approach is due end of 2026. Schools remain free to choose tools, including commercial ones. Sources: [AI Insider](https://aiinsider.nl/nieuws/kabinet-publieke-ai-omgeving-scholen-2028/), [Kennisnet](https://www.kennisnet.nl/nieuws/kennisnet-werkt-aan-plan-voor-publieke-ai-voorziening-in-po-en-vo/).

**Why it matters.** It is a structural bet that schools should not negotiate privacy, model behaviour and terms with each vendor separately. The letter reportedly notes that pupils who use AI to produce assignment text score lower on average, while AI used to learn works better.

**Context.** Follows Kennisnet's AI-literacy framework (10 Sept) and the EU AI Act's literacy obligation for school staff, and the broader Dutch push for public-values digitalisation.

**Watch next.** The end-2026 plan of approach: governance, who operates the hub, and how third-party tools connect. For lesson platforms like LessonUp this could become a gateway (or a gatekeeper): early engagement on integration standards and data terms looks worthwhile. Teachers gain from clearer, vetted tools; risk is slow delivery (2028) versus fast-moving commercial tools. Uncertainty: technical design is still undefined.

### 2. US "techlash": teachers, especially newer ones and in suburban/rural schools, doubt that tech helps learning (28-29 Oct)
**What happened.** EdWeek Research Center survey coverage reports that newer educators are less likely than veterans to say technology helps students learn, and that scepticism is strongest in suburban and rural schools. Sources: [Newer Educators Are Less Likely to Say Tech Helps Students Learn](https://www.edweek.org/technology/newer-educators-are-less-likely-to-say-tech-helps-students-learn/2026/10), [Techlash Is Strongest in Suburban and Rural Schools](https://www.edweek.org/technology/techlash-is-strongest-in-suburban-and-rural-schools/2026/10). (Based on EdWeek's summaries; I could not read the full articles or methodology.)

**Why it matters.** The generation that will staff schools for decades is the least convinced, which affects adoption of every digital tool, AI included.

**Context.** Phone bans, screen-time worries, mixed AI-tutoring evidence and cheating debates are feeding the shift. The same week EdWeek also ran a piece on AI halving IEP prep time, showing that value is accepted where it saves teacher time.

**Watch next.** Whether districts tighten procurement and demand evidence of impact. For edtech: lead with teacher time saved and pedagogical rationale, not features; measure learning outcomes.

### 3. Teacher workforce: Dutch evaluation of shortage measures; England delays retention scheme (26-27 Oct)
**What happened.** A Kamerbrief of 26 October from minister Letschert and state secretary Tielen announces a broad evaluation of what actually works against the teacher shortage, including support staff, differences between schools, and the effect of possible budget cuts; full report by December 2027 and no new conclusions yet ([HBP Media](https://hbpmedia.nl/nieuws/kabinet-onderzoekt-of-maatregelen-tegen-lerarentekort-daadwerkelijk-werken)). In England, the DfE reportedly delayed a flexible-working/retention programme by a year to autumn 2027 ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/teacher-retention-programme-delayed-by-a-year-dfe-reveals/); from search snippet, page not readable). The OECD's Education at a Glance 2026 (29 Sept) also focused on teacher shortages.

**Why it matters.** Both governments are buying time while shortages persist; evaluation to end-2027 means little new policy before then.

**Context.** The NEU strike ballot in England runs 3 Oct-15 Dec over pay and funding; the Dutch cao OMO 2026-2028 reached an agreement on 15 Oct.

**Watch next.** The NEU ballot result in December; whether the Dutch evaluation influences the budget debate. For teachers, workload is the pressure point; tools that cut preparation time are most relevant for lesson platforms.
