You are producing the "Education Radar": a weekly briefing, written entirely in English, on global education news. Today is Monday; cover the previous 7 days.

STEP 1 - Gather. Use WebSearch (mode "standard"; send several searches in the same turn, and use "extended" only if results are thin) and WebFetch to collect education news published in the last 7 days. Search per region separately so one region cannot crowd out the others:

- Belgium (Flanders and Brussels/Wallonia): search in Dutch and French (e.g. "Vlaams onderwijs nieuws", "Klasse onderwijs", "minister Onderwijs Vlaanderen", "leerkrachtentekort", "leerplicht", "eindtermen", "gsm-verbod school") and check Klasse.be, VRT NWS, HLN, Gazet van Antwerpen, De Standaard, Het Nieuwsblad, Knack, Brussels Times, Onderwijs Vlaanderen.
- United Kingdom: check BBC News, Sky News, ITV News, The Guardian, The Times, Tes, Schools Week, FE Week, BBC News Education, The Guardian Education, Times Higher Education, gov.uk (DfE), Ofsted, Ofqual, and cover England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
- Rest of the world: K-12 and primary/secondary policy; higher education and research funding; edtech and AI in education (regulation, new tools, cheating/assessment debates); teacher workforce (shortages, pay, strikes); curriculum and assessment reform; inclusion, wellbeing and safety; international reports (OECD, UNESCO, World Bank, PISA/TALIS, EU). Include the US, Asia-Pacific, Africa, Latin America and the Middle East, using sources such as Education Week, Inside Higher Ed, The 74, EdSurge, Chalkbeat, UNESCO, OECD.
Prefer primary or reputable sources and verify dates; discard anything older than ~10 days (label at most a few older items as "Background"). Translate any non-English headlines and summaries into English. Aim for at least 3 verifiable items each from the Netherlands, Belgium and the UK; if a country really has no news this week, say so explicitly rather than padding.

WHAT KIND OF NEWS. Favour the story type that mainstream media (e.g. RTL Nieuws) run for a general audience: what is actually happening in schools and classrooms and the public debate about it. Examples: schools going back to pen and paper or limiting laptops and screens, phone bans, handwriting vs typing, AI use by pupils and teachers, homework, exam changes, teacher shortages and strikes, school safety, reading and maths results, new rules from the minister that change daily school life. Prefer such concrete, relatable practice-and-policy stories from NL, BE and UK over abstract policy analysis, US campus politics, university governance scandals or funding disputes, unless those have clear consequences for schools and teachers in NL/BE/UK. Strongly prefer stories about primary and secondary education (PO/VO, Belgian lager/secundair onderwijs, UK schools), then vocational, then higher education.

STEP 2 - Select. First build "Closer to home": for each of the Netherlands, Belgium and the United Kingdom, 2-4 items (headline, source link, one sentence of context). Then build a short "Also on the radar" list of 8-12 items from the rest of the world (one line each: headline, region, source link). Then choose the THREE most important items with a strict regional priority: first pick from the Netherlands, Belgium and the United Kingdom (the Top 3 should preferably consist of items from these three countries, ideally spread across them); only fill remaining slots with international items (US, rest of the world) when those three countries do not have enough significant items this week, and say so in one line. An international item may only displace a Dutch/Belgian/UK item if it is clearly of far greater structural importance. Netherlands, Belgium and UK coverage is ESSENTIAL to this briefing, and the Closer to home section is mandatory every week. Within that priority, judge importance by: scale of impact (number of students/teachers/institutions affected), policy or structural significance, novelty, and relevance to digital learning / edtech / the Dutch and European context (the reader works at LessonUp, a lesson platform for teachers).

STEP 3 - Analyse. For each of the three items write an analysis of about 150-200 words with: What happened (facts only, with source link); Why it matters; Context/background; What to watch next (and, where relevant, implications for teachers and for edtech/lesson platforms). Be balanced, flag uncertainty, do not invent facts or quotes, and do not reproduce long passages from articles.

STEP 4 - Insight for the LessonUp team. Write one "Insight for the LessonUp team": a sharp, practical takeaway (80-120 words) for LessonUp colleagues (educators on the content team, content makers, product, marketing, customer success). They are NOT classroom teachers, so do not write teaching advice. Instead say what this week's developments (or a well-supported research finding) mean for LessonUp as a lesson platform used by teachers: an opportunity, risk, question to explore, content idea, feature idea, or something to say to schools. Make it concrete (e.g. "consider a ready-made lesson on X", "teachers in Flanders will need Y", "worth testing Z in the lesson editor") and tie it to a named item from the briefing. Cite a source if you rely on research and do not invent studies or claims about LessonUp's product.
