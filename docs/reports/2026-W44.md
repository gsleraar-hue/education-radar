# Education Radar - week 44, 2026-10-29

## Week in one glance
AI in schools dominated the debate: New York put a one-year pause on student AI use for schoolwork, which is now fuelling a Dutch discussion, while Dutch university colleges (hogescholen) were told to write their own AI rules. In the UK, the Budget's school-funding projections and the unresolved SEND funding plan set the tone. Belgium had a quiet week, with a strike at an Aalst primary school the main verifiable item.

*Note: search tools surfaced recent news unevenly this week. Coverage was built mainly from direct fetches of Tes, AOb, ScienceGuide, VRT, Inside Higher Ed and EdWeek; some items are dated by URL only.*

## Top 3

### 1. New York pauses AI for student schoolwork, and the Netherlands asks "what about us?" (Netherlands / US, K-12 and AI)
**What happened.** According to AOb's Onderwijsblad (24 Oct), New York has banned AI for schoolwork by students up to high-school age, will teach older students responsible use, bars teachers from using AI to grade work, and in effect pauses AI in schools for a year. In the Netherlands, the question is whether primary schools should follow; HvA lector Izaak Dekker called it sensible, while admitting a ban is hard to enforce and partly symbolic. [Source](https://www.aob.nl/onderwijsblad/rubrieken/new-york-verbiedt-gebruik-van-ai-voor-schoolwerk-en-nederland-dan/)

**Why it matters.** It is the clearest case of a large system choosing "test first, adopt later" for generative AI, and it lands as Dutch higher education is also pushed on AI rules (see below).

**Context.** Dekker argues that generic chatbots do what users ask, whereas education aims at independent thinking, and notes that earlier technologies (phones, whiteboards) were rolled out without proof of benefit. Per the AOb summary, research suggests AI can raise performance while undermining learning. This is one expert's view, not Dutch policy.

**What to watch.** Whether Dutch ministers or school boards pick up the idea, and how New York defines "schoolwork" in practice. For LessonUp: tools that keep pupils actively thinking (retrieval, discussion, teacher-led pacing) and make AI use transparent are better placed than tools that generate answers.

### 2. UK Budget: flat per-pupil funding, falling total spend, and no long-term SEND plan (United Kingdom, K-12 funding)
**What happened.** Tes's Budget digest reports OBR projections that mainstream school spending falls about 0.7% a year in real terms to 2031-32 (steeper from 2028-29), since per-pupil funding is flat while pupil numbers decline. Annual council SEND deficits are forecast to rise from £2.8bn (2025-26) to £5.7bn (2027-28), with the state taking over SEND funding from 2028-29. Inflation is forecast at 3.4% for 2027, so a planned 3% teacher pay rise could lag. [Budget digest](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/budget-2026-everything-schools-need-know) Separately, the f40 group warned DfE it needs a long-term SEND funding plan; schools see only one year of money from the £1.6bn inclusive mainstream fund. [Tes](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/dfe-warned-it-must-provide-long-term-send-funding-plan-for-schools)

**Why it matters.** Millions of pupils and every English school budget are affected, and SEND is the most acute structural pressure.

**Context.** These are projections, not confirmed cuts. DfE says it is putting over £5bn into SEND in core budgets and will clear 90% of approved deficits.

**What to watch.** The school funding settlement, the Teachers' Review Body remit (see UK section) and unions' response. For edtech: tighter budgets favour low-cost, inclusion-friendly tools and clear evidence of impact.

### 3. Dutch hogescholen told to set their own AI rules as the value of the diploma comes under pressure (Netherlands, higher education)
**What happened.** On 29 Oct the Vereniging Hogescholen warned that the hbo diploma's value is under pressure from AI, growing legalisation of exam committees' work, and flexibilisation of education. It says college boards must draw up rules for AI in teaching and assessment. [ScienceGuide](https://www.scienceguide.nl/2026/10/vereniging-hogescholen-hogeschoolbestuurders-moeten-ai-regels-opstellen/)

**Why it matters.** It moves AI from an individual teacher's dilemma to an institutional responsibility, with assessment integrity as the core issue.

**Context.** The same week, ScienceGuide ran an opinion piece arguing universities should not adapt teaching only out of fear of AI fraud. The industry view is not unanimous.

**What to watch.** Whether rules differ between institutions, and whether the debate reaches mbo and secondary education. For LessonUp: formative, in-lesson assessment that shows pupil thinking process can support the shift away from end-product-only grading.

## Education tip for the LessonUp team
**Start the lesson with a low-stakes retrieval question.** In a week where AI-versus-learning is the headline, retrieval practice is a reminder that effortful recall builds memory in a way that looking up an answer does not. Open with three questions on last lesson's content, answered individually before any discussion, with no notes and no AI. Evidence for the testing effect is robust (e.g. Roediger & Karpicke, 2006, *Psychological Science*). In an interactive lesson: a short open-question or multiple-choice slide where pupils answer on their own device, the teacher sees the spread of answers, then reveals the correct answer and discusses the common mistake. Keep it ungraded so errors are safe, and revisit missed items a week later.
